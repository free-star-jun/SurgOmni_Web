Abstract

TL;DR: SurgOmni is a unified benchmark for all-in-one surgical image restoration, featuring 44,000 paired training frames and 10,000 real-world evaluation frames with complex multi-degradations. Evaluations reveal limitations of generic IQA metrics and provide practical guidance for surgical image restoration.

Laparoscopic surgery relies on clear visualization for accurate decision-making, yet surgical videos are often degraded by smoke, fog, fluid, uneven illumination, and motion blur—often co-occurring in real procedures. To tackle the lack of paired supervision and the limitations of synthetic or pseudo-paired datasets, we introduce SurgOmni, a unified benchmark for all-in-one surgical image restoration under complex multi-degradation settings. It comprises OmniSyn, a degradation-aware paired training set with 44,000 high-resolution frames across 5 degradation types and 9 multi-degradation combinations, and OmniBench, a real-world evaluation benchmark with 10,000 frames spanning 6 surgical types, 10 organs, and 27 scenes with naturally co-occurring degradations. Extensive evaluations and surgeon assessments reveal inconsistencies among generic IQA metrics, validate OmniSyn’s effectiveness, and provide practical guidance for algorithm development and clinical dataset design.

Experiments & Analysis

The benchmark evaluates dataset quality, restoration fidelity, real-world perceptual quality, temporal behavior, downstream surgical perception, efficiency, and surgeon preference. These views intentionally remain separate because they favor different models.

E1
Training dataset comparison

OmniSyn transfers better under matched budgets

Against pseudo-paired SurgClean at 2,488 pairs, OmniSyn wins 126 of 135 real-world NR-IQA comparisons. Against exactly paired LVQ at 10,000 pairs, OmniSyn wins all 135 comparisons. Exact correspondence matters, but surgical-specific synthesis remains essential beyond exact pairing.

E2
Model benchmark

No single restorer dominates every criterion

SFHformer leads paired OmniSyn fidelity; OneRestore, BioIR, HOGformer, and UniRestore are stronger on real-domain perceptual metrics; DA-RCOT best preserves input-referenced motion on OmniBench; OneRestore is fastest and lowest-MAC; SFHformer has the fewest parameters.

Training split study

Composition should follow deployment conditions

single and mixed each use 160 clips, while scale combines both into 320 clips. Scale performs best overall on diverse Gallbladder and Colon domains. Under a fixed budget, mixed is advantageous on the narrower SurgClean domain because it concentrates smoke, haze, and splash exposure.

What the benchmark teaches us

Discussion: Path Towards All-in-One Surgical Restoration

01

Supervision is more than alignment

Exact correspondence and surgical-specific degradation modeling both determine real-world transfer. Pixel alignment alone does not make synthetic sources equivalent.

02

Quality is multi-objective

Paired fidelity, perceptual IQA, temporal agreement, downstream consistency, and surgeon judgment capture different behavior and favor different methods.

03

Strength depends on context

No model simultaneously leads fidelity, perception, structural preservation, temporal consistency, and efficiency. The intended clinical use should determine the trade-off.

04

Data should match deployment

Broader coverage is safer for diverse settings, while a fixed budget focused on dominant target degradations can improve domain-specific performance.

Surgical restoration should be developed by jointly considering supervision quality, degradation modeling, evaluation objectives, model capability, and target-aware data allocation—not by optimizing a single benchmark score.
Closing perspective

Conclusion

SurgOmni establishes a unified benchmark for all-in-one surgical restoration by combining multi-degradation paired training with authentic real-world clip-level evaluation. OmniSyn supplies exact, temporally coherent supervision for single and compound degradations, while OmniBench measures performance under naturally co-occurring degradations without artificial clean targets.

The experiments show that clinically useful restoration depends on more than reconstruction accuracy: models must balance perceptual quality, temporal behavior, anatomical structure, downstream consistency, efficiency, and deployment-aware training data. SurgOmni provides the data and evaluation structure needed to study these trade-offs systematically.

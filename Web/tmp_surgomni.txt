009                                                A BSTRACT
010
011            In laparoscopic surgery, clear visualization is critical for accurate intraoperative
012            decision-making, yet surgical videos are often degraded by surgical smoke, lens
013            fogging, fluid splashes, uneven illumination, and motion blur, which may even
014            co-occur in real procedures and make reliable restoration inherently challenging.
015            However, exact paired supervision is unavailable in real procedures because the
016            same evolving surgical scene cannot be captured simultaneously with and without
017            degradation. Real-world pseudo-paired datasets preserve authentic degradations
018
               but suffer from correspondence errors caused by optical-flow estimation, whereas
               synthetic datasets ensure pixel-level alignment but often oversimplify the physi-
019
               cal formation and co-occurrence of surgical degradations. To address these chal-
020
               lenges, we propose SurgOmni, a unified benchmark for training and evaluating
021            all-in-one surgical image restoration under complex multi-degradation settings.
022            Specifically, we construct OmniSyn, a degradation-aware paired training dataset
023            comprising 44,000 high-resolution frames across 5 degradation types and 9 multi-
024            degradation combinations. We further curate OmniBench, a real-world evaluation
025            benchmark containing 10,000 high-resolution frames spanning 6 surgical types,
026            10 target organs, and 27 scenes with diverse and naturally co-occurring degrada-
027            tions. Extensive objective evaluations and surgeon assessments reveal inconsisten-
028            cies among generic IQA metrics in surgical restoration, validate OmniSyn as an
029
               effective training dataset, and characterize model-specific strengths across diverse
               restoration settings and downstream tasks. Furthermore, training-split analysis
030
               shows that different configurations of degradation data offer distinct advantages
031
               across real-world conditions, providing practical guidance for all-in-one restora-
032            tion algorithm development and clinical dataset design. The datasets and code are
033            publicly available at https://github.com/Simon-leong/SurgOmni.

      1    I NTRODUCTION
037   Clear visualization is essential for reliable intraoperative perception in laparoscopic surgery, yet
038   surgical videos are frequently degraded by electrosurgical smoke, lens condensation, fluid splashes,
039   motion blur, and non-uniform illumination (Kubo et al., 2023; Pei et al., 2026). In surgical video,
040   these degradations arise from different stages of the imaging process and may occur simultaneously
041   in real procedures. Surgical restoration must therefore handle diverse and co-occurring degradations
042
      while preserving tissue boundaries, instrument structures, and other visual evidence required for
      surgical interpretation, motivating an all-in-one restoration setting beyond conventional degradation-
043
      specific solutions.
044
      However, existing surgical restoration remains largely degradation-specific, with most methods tar-
045
      geting individual factors such as surgical smoke (Chen et al., 2019; Venkatesh et al., 2020; Pan et al.,
046
      2022; Zhang et al., 2023; Li et al., 2024; Wu et al., 2024b). Although recent work has begun to con-
047   sider broader endoscopic restoration (Chen et al., 2026b; Pei et al., 2026) and all-in-one restoration
048   has advanced rapidly in natural imagery (Li et al., 2022; Potlapalli et al., 2023; Cui et al., 2025b; Tian
049   et al., 2025; Lin et al., 2026), extending this paradigm to surgical video remains constrained by the
050   lack of a reliable surgical restoration training dataset. Real datasets preserve authentic degradation
051   appearance, yet exact degraded-clean pairs are difficult to obtain from the same evolving surgical
052   scene, so existing supervision often relies on unpaired samples Pan et al. (2022); Wu et al. (2024b)
053   or temporally adjacent pseudo-targets that can retain correspondence errors (Xia et al., 2025; Pei
      et al., 2026). Synthetic datasets preserve exact pixel-level correspondence but often oversimplify

086   gical types, 10 target organs, and 27 scenes with naturally co-occurring multi-label degradations.

088   parisons with pseudo-paired SurgClean (Pei et al., 2026) and exactly paired LVQ (ICIP 2020 LVQ
089   Challenge Organizers, 2020) show that OmniSyn-trained models achieve stronger real-world trans-
090   fer across training sources. We then compare model behavior across degradation-specific evaluation,
091

092

093
      world conditions, providing practical guidance for all-in-one restoration algorithm development and
094
      clinical dataset design.

110   2.1    S URGICAL R ESTORATION DATASET
111
      Paired surgical restoration data are difficult to acquire because tissue state, viewpoint, instruments,
112   and illumination continuously change during surgery, making exact degraded-clean capture of
113   the same scene impractical. Real-world datasets therefore preserve authentic degradations at the
114   cost of exact correspondence, relying mainly on unpaired domains or temporally displaced refer-
115   ences. EAD (Ali et al., 2021) annotates multiple endoscopic degradations and supports degradation-
116   specific restoration without paired targets, while Cyclic-DesmokeGAN Venkatesh et al. (2020) and
117   DeSmoke-LAP Pan et al. (2022) adopt unpaired training, and LSVD Wu et al. (2024b) and Vivo
      Xia et al. (2024) construct pseudo-pairs from nearby frames with motion compensation. SurgClean
118
      (Pei et al., 2026) further broadens real-world coverage to smoke, haze, and splash, but still treats
119
      these degradations as separate restoration tasks and constructs approximate paired targets by align-
120   ing neighboring frames with optical flow. Synthetic datasets resolve the correspondence problem
121   by providing exact paired supervision, yet most existing surgical datasets remain limited to indi-
122   vidual degradations such as smoke or low light (Chen et al., 2019; Zhang et al., 2023; Liu et al.,
123   2024; Hong et al., 2023; Chang & Chen, 2024; Chen et al., 2024; Liang et al., 2026). The ICIP
124   LVQ challenge (ICIP 2020 LVQ Challenge Organizers, 2020) includes predefined multi-degradation
125   mixtures, but uses simplified frame-level operators developed for quality assessment and recognition
      rather than surgical restoration. Therefore, existing datasets remain limited in jointly providing exact
126
      correspondence, surgical-specific degradation modeling, and broad multi-degradation coverage.
127
128
      2.2    S URGICAL A LL - IN -O NE R ESTORATION
129
130   Surgical restoration remains largely degradation-specific, with most work focusing on smoke re-
131
      moval through cooperative generation, unpaired translation, frequency processing, and temporal
      modeling (Chen et al., 2019; Venkatesh et al., 2020; Pan et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2023; Li et al.,
132
      2024; Wu et al., 2024b; Liang et al., 2026). EndoIR (Chen et al., 2026b) is the closest model-
133   centric counterpart to the all-in-one setting, handling co-occurring endoscopic degradations through
134   noise-aware diffusion. In natural imagery, all-in-one restoration has progressed from degradation
135   representations and learned prompts to frequency modulation, feature perturbation, and expert rout-
136   ing (Li et al., 2022; Potlapalli et al., 2023; Cui et al., 2025b; Tian et al., 2025; Lin et al., 2026),
137   while vision-language and generative priors further extend restoration through multimodal condi-
138   tioning and pretrained image generators (Luo et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2025; Rajagopalan et al.,
139
      2026). Recent work has also expanded the problem toward composite and time-varying degrada-
      tions (Guo et al., 2024; Zhao et al., 2024), while GenDeg Rajagopalan et al. (2025) and FoundIR
140
      Li et al. (2025) explore degradation synthesis and large-scale paired training, and FoundIR-v2 Chen
141   et al. (2026c) further studies the effect of training-data mixtures. Despite these advances, surgical
142   all-in-one restoration remains underexplored in real procedures, where degradations arise from dis-
143   tinct formation mechanisms, naturally co-occur, and evolve over time, while exact clean supervision
144   remains unavailable.

149   Let D = {smoke, haze, splash, blur, NUI} denote the surgical degradation set, where NUI repre-
150   sents non-uniform illumination, and let S ⊆ D denote the active degradations. Given a clean clip
151   x1:T , OmniSyn generates:
152                                  y1:T = GS (x1:T ; ϕ),       x⋆1:T = x1:T                             (1)
153
154   where GS denotes the degradation renderer conditioned on S, ϕ represents its rendering parameters
155
      and temporal states, y1:T is the degraded clip, and x⋆1:T is the paired clean reference. We define
      the five degradations according to their physical origins in the surgical imaging process. Smoke is a
156
      scene-space aerosol, haze is lens-adjacent condensation, splash is adherent fluid with local refraction
157   and occlusion, blur results from exposure integration of camera, tissue, or instrument motion, and
158   NUI covers spatially varying over- and under-illumination. The same taxonomy is used throughout
159   SurgOmni, providing a consistent basis for controlled degradation synthesis in OmniSyn and multi-
160   label annotation of naturally occurring degradations in OmniBench. Table 1 compares SurgOmni
161   with existing surgical restoration datasets, and Appendix A provides detailed degradation definitions
      and rendering mechanisms.

      3.2 O MNI S YN : P HYSICS -G UIDED PAIRED T RAINING DATASET
179
180
      We curate temporally continuous clean reference clips from CholecTrack20 Nwoye et al. (2025)
      and M2CAI16-TOOL Twinanda et al. (2016), while preserving natural camera, tissue, and instru-
181
      ment motion. Rather than independently perturbing individual frames, OmniSyn synthesizes each
182   degradation according to its formation mechanism in surgical imaging, as illustrated in Figure 2.
183   Smoke is modeled as dynamic scene-space aerosol with diffusion, ambient, and entangled patterns
184   Liang et al. (2026), while haze and splash are rendered as lens-adjacent effects using droplet geom-
185   etry, scattering, refraction, transparency, and absorption. Motion blur is generated through temporal
186   densification and exposure integration, whereas NUI combines content-aware highlight expansion
187   with spatially varying low-light attenuation. These mechanisms evolve with temporally consistent
188
      states, allowing smoke and fluid to follow continuous trajectories while lens-attached and illumina-
      tion effects remain consistent with their respective coordinate systems. Compound samples com-
189
      bine multiple degradation mechanisms on the same clean sequences, producing diverse dual and
190   triple-degradation interactions while retaining an exact clean reference. After automatic integrity
191   checks and multi-stage visual inspection, OmniSyn contains 44,000 paired high-resolution frames
192   in 440 clips, including 240 single, 162 dual, and 38 triple-degradation clips. Detailed rendering
193   mechanisms, parameter ranges, quality-control procedures, and dataset distributions are provided in
194   Appendix A.
195   3.3 O MNI B ENCH : R EAL -W ORLD S URGICAL V IDEO B ENCHMARK
196
      OmniBench is designed to evaluate restoration under authentic surgical degradations. We com-
197
      bine public cholecystectomy videos with a newly curated clinical colorectal cohort from a hospital,
198   extending the benchmark beyond existing public datasets and substantially increasing its procedu-
199   ral, anatomical, and degradation diversity. To prevent source overlap with OmniSyn, all clips are
200   selected from surgical scenes that are disjoint from the clean source videos used for OmniSyn con-
201   struction. Candidate videos are reviewed at the clip level, where temporally continuous segments
202   are selected only when visual interference is clearly identifiable while sufficient anatomical or in-
203   strumental information remains for restoration assessment. Rather than assigning a single dominant
      degradation, all visible factors are retained through clip-level multi-label annotation to preserve
204
      their natural co-occurrence in real surgery. Each retained clip undergoes independent verification
205
      for degradation visibility, label completeness, temporal continuity, surgical relevance, and restora-
206   tion feasibility. The resulting benchmark contains 10,000 high-resolution frames in 100 clips across
207   6 surgical types, 10 target organs, and 27 scenes, including 43 isolated, 45 dual, and 12 triple-
208   degradation clips. OmniBench is reserved exclusively for evaluation and is never used for training
209   or model selection. Detailed data collection, sampling criteria, annotation guidelines, verification
210   procedures, and dataset statistics are provided in Appendix B.

285   4.2.1                         DATASET C OMPARISON
286
287
      To validate the effectiveness of OmniSyn as a training dataset, we compare it with SurgClean and
      LVQ under two matched-budget settings across 15 restoration methods. In the 2,488-pair compari-
288
      son with SurgClean, OmniSyn achieves higher real-world NR-IQA scores in 126 of 135 individual
289   comparisons across the evaluated models, domains, and IQA metrics, including 40 of 45 compar-
290   isons on SurgClean’s own test set. This consistent advantage supports the importance of accurate
291   correspondence, as optical-flow-based pseudo-pairs in SurgClean can retain residual spatial mis-
292   alignment between degraded inputs and reference frames. We further compare OmniSyn with LVQ
293   using 10,000 exactly paired samples from each dataset with matched counts across their shared
294   degradation categories, where OmniSyn achieves higher NR-IQA scores than LVQ in all 135 real-
295
      world comparisons. Since both sources provide exact paired supervision, this result shows that
      correspondence alone does not make synthetic training sources equivalent in real-world transfer.
296
      It further indicates that the design of the synthetic training source remains important beyond ex-
297   act pairing, with OmniSyn retaining its advantage on the external Colon and SurgClean domains.
298   The same overall direction is observed across convolutional, transformer, state-space, and diffusion
299   architectures, indicating that the training-source effect is not confined to a particular model family.

301   4.2.2                         M ODEL C OMPARISON
302
      Quantitative Analysis. Table 2 compares 15 restoration methods trained on single-degradation
303   samples, while Figure 4 further examines their performance across different degradation categories
304   on OmniBench. A clear distinction emerges between paired reconstruction fidelity and real-world
305   perceptual quality. SFHformer Jiang et al. (2024) achieves the best PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS on
306   paired OmniSyn, whereas the leading methods on real surgical scenarios shift toward OneRestore
307   Guo et al. (2024), BioIR Cui et al. (2025a), HOGformer Wu et al. (2026), and UniRestore Chen et al.
308   (2025). The degradation-conditioned results reinforce this evaluator dependence: UniRestore ranks
309
      first under TOPIQ across all 10 degradation categories after equally weighting the two OmniBench
      domains, while BioIR and HOGformer remain stronger under MUSIQ and Q-Align. EndoIR Chen
310
      et al. (2026b) and RAR Chen et al. (2026a) lag on paired fidelity and real-world IQA, with their na-
311   tive low-resolution processing potentially limiting high-resolution surgical detail preservation. Clip-
312   level motion analysis introduces another distinction that frame-level quality metrics cannot capture.
313   SFHformer achieves the lowest clean-referenced tOF on paired OmniSyn, while DA-RCOT Tang
314   et al. (2025) achieves the lowest input-referenced tOF on both real OmniBench subsets, despite nei-
315   ther method consistently dominating the perceptual IQA metrics. In particular, UniRestore remains
316   strongly favored by TOPIQ but shows substantially larger input-referenced motion deviation, high-
317
      lighting the value of clip-level evaluation even for frame-wise restorers. Efficiency adds a further
      trade-off, where OneRestore attains the lowest MACs and latency, whereas SFHformer uses the

455   4.4                        D ISCUSSION : PATH TOWARDS A LL - IN -O NE S URGICAL R ESTORATION
456
      Our experiments reveal four principles for all-in-one surgical restoration. (1) Effective supervision
457
      depends on both correspondence quality and surgical-specific degradation modeling. OmniSyn
458
      outperforms pseudo-paired SurgClean and exactly paired LVQ under matched training budgets,
459
      showing that exact correspondence alone does not determine real-world transfer and that degra-
460
      dation construction remains an important part of the supervision design. (2) Restoration quality
461
      cannot be characterized by a single evaluation criterion. Paired fidelity, perceptual IQA, tempo-
462
      ral agreement, and downstream consistency favor different methods, and clip-level temporal eval-
463
      uation further captures behavior that frame-level quality metrics cannot reveal. Visually preferred
464
      restoration therefore does not necessarily preserve motion or task-relevant structures required by
465
      downstream surgical perception tasks. (3) Model strengths depend on the evaluation setting. Dif-
466
      ferent methods exhibit distinct advantages in fidelity, perception, structural preservation, temporal
467
      consistency, and efficiency, leaving no single method consistently dominant across all criteria. Prac-
468
      tical restoration systems therefore need to balance these objectives according to their intended use.
469
      (4) Training-data composition should reflect deployment conditions. Broader training cover-
470
      age provides a stronger general-purpose choice across diverse surgical settings, while concentrating
471
      a fixed training budget on degradations that dominate the target domain can yield better domain-
472
      specific performance. These results suggest that surgical restoration should be developed by jointly
473
      considering supervision quality, degradation modeling, evaluation objectives, model capability, and
474
      target-aware data allocation rather than optimizing a single benchmark score.

478
479   We introduce SurgOmni, a unified benchmark for all-in-one surgical restoration that combines multi-
480   degradation paired training with authentic real-world evaluation. OmniSyn provides exact and tem-
481   porally coherent supervision across single and compound degradations, while OmniBench supports
482   clip-level evaluation under naturally co-occurring multi-degradations. Extensive experiments show
483   that effective surgical restoration depends not only on accurate supervision and degradation model-
484   ing, but also on balancing perceptual quality, temporal and structural preservation, and target-aware
485   data composition. These findings provide practical guidance for developing restoration systems that
      improve surgical visibility while preserving task-relevant information.

812   A.1      C LEAN C LIP C URATION AND P REPROCESSING
813
814   OmniSyn is built from temporally continuous clean sequences in CholecTrack20 and M2CAI16-
815   TOOL (Nwoye et al., 2025; Twinanda et al., 2016). Selection is performed over four-second win-
816   dows rather than isolated frames. We exclude intervals containing visible smoke, lens fogging,
817
      adherent liquid, pronounced motion or defocus blur, severe camera shake, large-area obstruction,
      exposure failure, or persistent loss of anatomical visibility. Natural viewpoint changes, instrument
818
      motion, tissue deformation, and mild specular reflection are retained when the operative field re-
819
      mains interpretable. This criterion preserves genuine surgical dynamics without admitting the target
820   degradation into the clean reference. FFmpeg normalizes frame rate and encoding (FFmpeg Devel-
821   opers, 2026a). Invalid peripheral regions are estimated once for each clip, removed by a fixed crop,
822   and followed by proportional resizing and center cropping to 1080 × 720. The same transform is
823   applied throughout the sequence and to both members of every pair.
